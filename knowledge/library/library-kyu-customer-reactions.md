## 2. THE IDEA IN ONE SENTENCE

A display of gifts in the store, then a move to customers' doors to show the delivery and their reactions.

## 3. HOOK (0–3 s)

0–2: two people next to a large TV box outdoors; 2–3: back to a presenter in front of gifts in the store. H03 primary and H04 secondary; a shot from the outcome precedes the explanation.

## 4. TIME STRUCTURE

0–2: setup from an interview; 2–22: gifts, a table and a large box inside the store, with explanatory graphics; 23–45: a night visit and handing over a box; 46–61: a second customer; 62–83: handing over a TV and an interaction in front of a door.

## 5. STRATEGY

[ASSUMPTION] Turning the app's benefit into a visible event and a person's experience. Details of the prizes and conditions are not established by this research.

## 6. CHARACTERS

A young presenter with glasses, a dark shirt and light trousers, and various customers. Not every presenter on Mohammed Omar's channel is Mohammed Omar. Customers' faces are not added to the character board.

## 7. CREATIVE MECHANISM

Early outcome → explanation → social proof through the scene. White boxes with red ribbons make the benefit tangible.

## 8. COMEDY AND EMOTION

Expressions of surprise, smiles and handing over by hand in the frames. No judgment is made on the genuineness or spontaneity of the reactions without additional evidence.

## 9. CRAFT

9:16; a store aisle, then handheld night footage at the doors; changing light color; short texts identifying the speakers. Audio saved; tone and laughter were not examined by listening.

## 10. ENDING AND ENGAGEMENT

Ends at the TV handover and the interaction; a link to the app is visible in the top text. No documented commercial result.

## 11. ASSESSMENT

[ASSUMPTION] Possible impact: outcome first; size of the gift; multiple reactions. Risks: incomplete offer conditions, customers' faces, and scenes at home doorways.

## 12. WHAT TRANSFERS

Make the promise something a person receives and reacts to; use actors and an original situation instead of carrying over customers' identities or a home address.

## 13. AI PRODUCTION FEASIBILITY

A precise hand-to-hand handover is harder than a close-up reaction. Better to split it into a separate box shot and an expression shot, with a limit of two characters.

## 14. COMPLIANCE

C1: faces of ordinary people in a home setting; the research does not permit reusing them. Do not extract their addresses or account screens. C2/C3: packaging and offers need permission and confirmation.
