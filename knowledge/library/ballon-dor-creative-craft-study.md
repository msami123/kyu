# The Ballon d'Or: A Study of Character Writing, Jokes and Details

## The reference's purpose

The client's direction on 2026-11-09: study the level of creativity and specificity in the characters' jokes and the details, to build the ability to create original work with the same level of care. **The Ballon d'Or is a craft standard, not a story template for Kyu.** A heist, a chase, a trophy, or moving the event to a supermarket is not automatically derived from it. The shared event puts pressure on the characters; the difference in their responses produces the jokes. It isn't enough to describe everyone as "funny" or "excited".

## 2. The running joke changes with each return

Vini's case is a clear example: the setup is personal at the door; the return happens in the middle of a different mission; then the broadcast repeats the misrecognition in front of a wider audience. The payoff isn't the repetition of the line alone: **who does the denying changes, the scope of the embarrassment widens, and correcting it becomes harder.** [Evidence 4–10, 127–136 seconds; LIB:library-ballon-dor-heist.md#4-time-structure]

[ASSUMPTION] Writing lesson: when bringing a joke back, change at least the source of the pressure, its cost, its context, or the result of the character's attempt to fix it. Don't make repetition a slogan that's repeated without development. Imitating the non-recognition joke is not what's required; what's required is inventing a problem that belongs to our character, from within their world.

## 4. The joke affects the event

The selfie delays Jude. Dembélé turning to the coverage conflicts with his mission. The intervention of the character who is outside the chase changes the outcome. The bus closes a path and opens the question of the next solution. [Evidence: 45–57, 65–71, 99–106, 122–126 seconds]

[ASSUMPTION] A practical test: if we delete the joke, what changes in our understanding of the character or the course of the scene? If the answer is "nothing", it may be a superficial addition. This is an editorial question, not a ban on a short standalone joke; some comedic breathers are useful.

[ASSUMPTION] The right level of ambition: a strong moment can reveal a character, move an event, and bring back an old detail at the same time. We don't forcibly cram three functions into every shot; we look for relationships that make the functions come together naturally.

## 5. Irony comes from the difference in what the parties know

At the selfie we see both the urgency of the mission and the time being wasted on it. With Vini we follow his insistence on identifying himself while the channel describes him differently. And when some of those present become spectators, a crisis for the pursuers and entertainment for the seats coexist. [Evidence: 45–57, 127–136, 148–156 seconds]

[ASSUMPTION] Writing lesson: define what the audience knows, what the character expects, and what a third party sees. The difference between these layers creates irony even if nobody says a "gag". We don't confine comedic writing to dialogue.

## 6. The shot needs a clear action and a point of view

In the same example we see the formality of the clothes and the ceremony alongside small behaviors that derail the heroics: wanting a photo, turning toward a lens, confusion about identity. This legible contrast helps bring together the big world and small human weakness. [LIB:library-ballon-dor-heist.md#9-craft]

[ASSUMPTION] Execution standard: we choose the framing size, the angle and the visual detail that make the irony understandable; a beautiful shot alone is not proof of the joke's quality. The reaction can be the end of the joke, so it needs its own space. The length of that space and the way of the vocal performance were not settled in this review.

## 7. How we use the study in original ideas

These are editorial working suggestions [ASSUMPTION] responding to the client's direction; they do not choose a concept or give a PASS in place of the Critic.

Before writing dialogue, one can define for each character: a goal in the situation, an image they want to maintain in front of others, a temptation or weak spot that derails the goal, a distinctive way of acting under pressure, and the effect of their action on someone else. The difference lies in behavior and choice, not only clothes and names.

Then we check the draft with these questions:

1. Which joke doesn't work the same way if we swap its owner for another character? And do the supporting characters perform clear functions?
2. What specific local observation produced the idea? And does it feel like it springs from the people and the place?
3. Where was the detail planted, and how did its meaning or use change when it returned?
4. How does the moment of laughter affect the action, the relationship, or the viewer's expectation?
5. Does the surprise have a reason that can be understood after it happens, or was it added arbitrarily?
6. Does the execution make the idea easy to read, and is there room for the reaction?
7. If we hid the reference's names and images, would our idea still look like a copy of its story? If yes, we go back to a different original observation.
8. Why wouldn't our story happen with the same meaning without Kyu? We answer from an approved brand truth, not by pasting its logo onto a ready-made idea. [BRAND:Offer and products]

We don't use the number of jokes or the size of the scene as an automatic measure of creativity. A small idea with two characters can reach this level of precision. And we don't assume that the loudest characters are the best, or that every film needs a chase and an escalation in scale.

## 8. What has been learned and what remains open

- **High:** the differing functions of the characters; the recognition joke repeating with a changing context; the reuse of the horse, the screen and the bus; the presence of consequences for some comedic moments. The temporary evidence is set out above.
- **Medium:** interpreting this as a conflict between the goal and status/temptation, and its likely role in the work's cohesion. This is an editorial reading [ASSUMPTION].
- **Incomplete:** references that require football knowledge outside the text, audio jokes, the exact timing of pauses and music, and the delivery of lines. We don't claim to have decoded all the layers of the work before examining them.
